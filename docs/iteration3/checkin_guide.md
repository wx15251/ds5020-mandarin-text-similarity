# Iteration 3 explanation guide

Use this with the report and printed tiny example. These are preparation notes, not a record that James has already reviewed or presented the work.

1. **What does A mean?** It has one term per row and one document per column. Entry (t,j) is the count of term t in document j multiplied by log(n/df_t). The real character matrix is 3,123 x 300; the word matrix is 19,800 x 300.
2. **Why A transpose A?** For any x, x-transpose A-transpose A x is the squared length of Ax, so the Gram matrix is symmetric positive semidefinite. Its nonnegative eigenvalues are squared singular values. The smaller equivalent Gram saves work.
3. **How does power iteration work?** Repeated multiplication amplifies the leading eigen-direction relative to smaller ones. Normalize each step. Convergence is slower when the first two eigenvalues are close. Reorthogonalization removes numerical contamination from directions already found.
4. **What is deflation?** Subtract lambda v v-transpose from the Gram matrix after finding a unit eigenvector v. This removes that exact direction and leaves orthogonal eigenvectors unchanged. The implementation checks residuals and raises an error on nonconvergence.
5. **What happens in the toy example?** The two singular values are log(3/2) times sqrt(6) and sqrt(2). Keeping only the first retains 75% of energy but makes all three documents positive scalar coordinates. Their reduced cosines are all one, including the originally orthogonal first and third documents.
6. **Why two query maps?** Projected query z=U-transpose q belongs with document rows V Sigma. Dividing z by the singular values gives folded coordinates that belong with V alone. Dividing axes by different amounts changes angles; do not mix the pairs.
7. **How was k chosen?** Take the smallest k with sum of retained sigma squared divided by total Frobenius energy at least 0.90. The chosen ranks are 137 and 176. The previous ranks fall below the target. Energy controls reconstruction error, not relevance.
8. **What is truth in the simulation?** Topic labels and the chosen topic term probabilities are known. We check topic recovery and pooled first-half term probabilities. We do not compare arbitrary SVD vectors with a fabricated true basis.
9. **Why does the error plot fluctuate?** Each point is one random sample. The theoretical SE shrinks as 1/sqrt(n), while an individual absolute error can go up. All six probability estimates lie within three true standard errors here.
10. **What does the stress test show?** Mixing both distributions toward their average removes their distinction. The within/across cosine gap vanishes even while custom/library agreement remains excellent. A correct solver cannot recover topic information that the model no longer supplies.
11. **Did SVD improve the real results?** It increased both within and across cosine means and increased their gap. Character nearest-topic agreement stayed at 92%; word agreement fell from about 94.3% to 89.7%. The query results also contain clear relevance failures. Explain both outcomes.
12. **Why can library factors differ?** Singular-vector signs are arbitrary, and repeated singular values permit rotated bases. Compare singular values, reconstructed matrices, projectors and cosines rather than requiring arbitrary raw vectors to match.
13. **What limits the study?** The sample comes from a finite source prefix. Topic labels are a proxy. Bag representations discard order; names and document length can dominate. Related articles may inflate nearest-topic scores. This is in-corpus descriptive retrieval, not held-out generalization.
